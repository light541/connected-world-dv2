Small devices. Enormous reach. From internet access to mobile subscriptions and the electronic waste they leave behind, the geography of consumer technology tells a story of opportunity and inequality.

Four questions, one connected story.Who can get online? How widespread is cellular service? Which vendors lead in different markets? And what happens to the resulting electronic waste?

A public-interest visual essay for a Malaysian audience. Data years are labelled separately because the underlying statistics are published at different intervals.

CHAPTER 04 / THE AFTERLIFE

The other side of consumption.

Phones, computers and other electronics eventually reach the end of their lives. The formal recycling share measures the fraction of recorded electronic waste entering documented recycling systems — a narrower idea than all reuse, recovery, or informal handling.

Final perspective

Connection is not the full story.

Connectivity, device access and formal recycling cannot be collapsed into one ranking. These indicators measure different things, in different years. Viewed together, they invite a more responsible question: how do we expand access to technology while designing for longer device lifetimes and better recovery?

DATA & METHODOLOGY

Sources, definitions and limitations

The website first reads local CSV snapshots of published Our World in Data observations; if a required local CSV is missing, it falls back to the linked publisher URL. Records with absent or invalid values are excluded, never imputed. Dataset years and country coverage differ, so figures should not be combined into a single causal claim. Check source pages for later revisions.

  • Individuals using the internet — ITU World Telecommunication/ICT Indicators Database, via World Bank and OWID. Percent of population.
  • Mobile phone subscriptions per 100 people — ITU via World Bank and OWID. Subscriptions, not unique smartphone owners or devices sold.
  • Electronic waste recycling rate — UN Statistics Division and UNEP via OWID. Formal collection/recycling of all e-waste, not specifically phones.
  • Population by country — UN World Population Prospects via OWID, used only in the treemap’s transparent population × share estimate.
  • Mobile vendor browsing share (September 2026) — Statcounter Global Stats, with individual source pages for Malaysia, Asia, Europe, India and the United States. The bundled CSV transcribes each page’s six displayed vendors; unlisted brands are not imputed.
  • World map geometry — Vega Datasets; country coordinates are approximate geographic references from the CountryInfo package.

Reading the charts: 2024 indicators are not claimed to describe smartphone sales or ownership; some countries lack data. The tile-grid is a schematic region map whose acceptance as a third map idiom should be confirmed with the tutor. The year of the 2022 recycling observations differs from the year of the 2024 connectivity observations. Circle areas denote subscription rates. The treemap contains only the largest paired 2023 estimates. Bundled CSV snapshots are preferred when available. Internet access is still required for chart libraries and geographic geometry loaded from public CDNs. The September 2026 Statcounter dataset contains only each market’s six listed vendors; unlisted vendors are not zero.
